Basophils are the rarest type of white blood cell. They make up less than 1 percent of all white blood cells in a healthy person.

They play a role in allergic reactions and in signalling other immune cells when there is a threat. When activated, they release a chemical called histamine, which is what causes the itching, swelling, and redness you feel during an allergic reaction.

They are included in the CBC as part of the complete differential count. Although a very small number, they become significant when their count rises, which can sometimes be associated with certain blood conditions or severe allergic responses.

Your basophil level is [VALUE] percent of your white blood cells. The normal range is 0.5 to 1 percent.

Basophils are almost never the main concern in a routine CBC. A slightly raised value on its own is usually not a cause for worry unless other values are also outside the normal range.
