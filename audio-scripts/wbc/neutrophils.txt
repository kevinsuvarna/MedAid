Neutrophils are the most common type of white blood cell. They are your body's first responders.

When bacteria or a fungal infection enters your body, neutrophils rush to the site within minutes. They surround and destroy the harmful cells before they can spread. They are the reason a wound becomes slightly red and warm when it is healing.

They are measured in the CBC because a high neutrophil count is one of the clearest signs of a bacterial infection or inflammation somewhere in the body. A low count can mean the body is struggling to defend itself.

Your neutrophil level is [VALUE] percent of your white blood cells. The normal range is 40 to 70 percent.

A temporary rise in neutrophils is common and usually nothing to worry about. If the level stays high, your doctor will look for the source of the infection or inflammation.
