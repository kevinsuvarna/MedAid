Lymphocytes are the second most common white blood cell. They are your body's long-term memory fighters.

There are two main types. B-lymphocytes make antibodies, which are proteins that remember past infections and stop them from making you sick again. T-lymphocytes directly attack cells that have been infected by viruses. Together, they are the reason vaccines work.

Lymphocytes are measured in the CBC because a high count can be a sign of a viral infection like dengue or COVID, or sometimes a blood-related condition. A low count can mean the immune system is under stress or being suppressed.

Your lymphocyte level is [VALUE] percent of your white blood cells. The normal range is 20 to 40 percent.

Lymphocyte levels often change during and after an illness. Your doctor will look at the pattern over time rather than a single reading.
