MCH stands for Mean Corpuscular Haemoglobin. It tells us the average amount of haemoglobin inside each of your red blood cells.

Remember that haemoglobin is the protein that carries oxygen. MCH tells us whether each individual red blood cell is carrying enough of it.

This is measured because it helps doctors understand the quality of each red blood cell, not just how many there are. A cell can look normal in size but still carry too little oxygen if the haemoglobin inside it is low.

Your MCH is [VALUE] picograms. The normal range is 27 to 33 picograms.

A low MCH often goes together with low MCV and low haemoglobin. Together, these three numbers help your doctor understand the full picture of your red blood cells.
