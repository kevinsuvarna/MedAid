PCV stands for Packed Cell Volume. It is also called Haematocrit, or HCT.

This number tells us what percentage of your blood is made up of red blood cells. For example, a PCV of 40 means that 40 out of every 100 parts of your blood are red blood cells.

It is measured because it gives doctors a quick picture of whether you have enough red blood cells overall. It works together with your haemoglobin reading to confirm whether anaemia is present.

Your PCV is [VALUE] percent. The normal range is 40 to 50 percent for men and 36 to 46 percent for women.

If this number is low, it usually means the same thing as low haemoglobin. Your doctor will look at both together to understand your situation.
