MCHC stands for Mean Corpuscular Haemoglobin Concentration. It tells us how concentrated, or packed, the haemoglobin is inside your red blood cells.

Think of it like this. MCH tells you the total amount of haemoglobin in one cell. MCHC tells you how densely it is packed inside that cell. A cell could have a normal amount of haemoglobin but be very large, making it less concentrated.

This value is in the CBC report because it helps detect specific types of anaemia. Very low MCHC can indicate iron deficiency. Very high MCHC can sometimes point to a condition where red blood cells break down too easily.

Your MCHC is [VALUE] grams per decilitre. The normal range is 32 to 36 grams per decilitre.

Doctors usually look at MCHC together with MCV and MCH for a complete picture. On its own it rarely causes concern.
